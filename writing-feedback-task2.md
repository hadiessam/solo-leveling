## ERROR LIST

### 1. TENSE — the same problem as Task 1

This is a **story about something that already happened**, so every verb must be past.
You wrote:

| You wrote | Should be |
|---|---|
| they **start** moving | they **started** moving |
| and **talk** to their friends | and **talked** to their friends |
| how they **being invited** | how they **had been** invited |
| Concert host **take** the microphone | the concert host **took** the microphone |
| and **start** to talk | and **started** to talk |
| how he **enjoy** his time | how he **had enjoyed** his time |
| and **continue** his talk | and **continued** his talk |
| saying **its** like a dream | saying it **was** like a dream |

**Notice:** you got the FIRST sentence right — *"arrived"*, *"couldn't believe"*. Both past.
Then from the second sentence onward, everything flipped to present tense.

That is a very specific and very fixable habit: you start in the past, then drift.
**The drill: after you finish writing, go back and circle every verb. Any verb that is
not past → fix it.** That single check would have caught all eight of these.

### 2. "its" vs "it's" vs "it was"

You wrote: *"saying its like a dream"*

- **its** = belonging to it (the dog wagged **its** tail)
- **it's** = it is
- You needed: *"saying it **was** like a dream"*

Never write "its" unless you mean possession.

### 3. MISSING ARTICLE

| You wrote | Should be |
|---|---|
| Concert host take the microphone | **the** concert host took the microphone |

**Rule:** a singular countable noun in English almost always needs "a", "an", "the" or
"my/your/his". "Concert host" alone is not possible in English.

### 4. WORDS THAT ARE NOT QUITE ENGLISH

| You wrote | Natural English |
|---|---|
| moving **in** the concert | moving **around** the concert / the venue |
| all the **attended** members | all the **people who attended** / the **attendees** |
| **in this concert** (x2) | **at the concert** |
| they **found** all their friends | they **saw** all their friends |

**"at the concert"** — you go **to** a concert but you are **at** a concert. Small word,
big difference in how native it sounds.

### 5. REPEATED WORDS

| Word | Times |
|---|---|
| and | 11 |
| concert | 6 |
| all | 4 |
| talk | 4 |

Some repetition is natural in a 100-word text, but **6 × "concert"** is a lot. Once you
have said "the concert", you can say **"it"**, **"the venue"**, or **"the show"**.

### 6. THE FIRST SENTENCE HAS A COMMA SPLICE

```
All and Nicky arrived at the concert and couldn't believe their eyes, they found all their friends in this concert.
```

Two complete sentences joined by a comma. In English that is an error (a "comma splice").
Options:
- Full stop: *"...couldn't believe their eyes. They found all their friends there."*
- Or a connector: *"...couldn't believe their eyes **because** they found all their friends there."*

Also: the prompt gave you **"Ali and Nicky"** and you wrote **"All and Nicky"**.
Check names against the prompt — easy marks to lose.
