How to Actually Get Something Out of a Book

READ THIS FIRST
Reading a book is not the goal. Changing how you work is the goal. These are the rules that make the difference between finishing a book and using it.
1
Read with a pen, never without
Underline. Write in the margin. A book you have written in is a book you will use. A clean book is a book you will forget.
2
Stop at every idea and say it in your own words
If you cannot explain it back to yourself, you did not understand it. Close the book, say it out loud, then open it again. This single habit is worth more than reading ten books.
3
Turn one idea into one action before you read the next
Do not finish a book with 40 highlights and no change. Pick one thing and do it this week. One used idea beats fifty collected ones.
4
For English books: read at 95% understanding, not 100%
If you need a dictionary for every third word, the book is too hard. If you need it once a page, it is perfect. Stop looking up every unknown word — guess from context, check later.
5
Read the same paragraph out loud once
Reading silently trains reading. Reading out loud trains speaking, pronunciation and rhythm. Your speaking is 50/B1 and needs the work — every book is a speaking exercise if you let it be.
6
Summarise the chapter in three sentences — in English
This is the most valuable habit on this list for you. Three sentences, written down, in English. It trains writing (your A2 gap), comprehension and recall all at once. Put them in a notebook.
7
Re-read the best book twice
The second read of a good book teaches more than the first read of a new one. The only books worth re-reading are the ones you acted on the first time.
The trap: it feels productive to collect books, download them, and mark them "read". It is not. You are not building a library. You are building a skill set. One book, applied, is worth a hundred on this list.
